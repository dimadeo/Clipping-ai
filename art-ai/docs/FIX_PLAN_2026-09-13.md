# The Dark Matters — Fix Plan

**Date:** 2026-09-13
**Basis:** the 2026-09-12 audit, five component designs, and six adversarial verifications (feasibility, regression, efficacy) that ran before the session limit. Nine verifications and the integration step did not run; I integrated by hand and adopted every high or critical finding the verifiers produced.
**Brief received:** a four-piece connected collection of USA landscapes, saturated sunset palette, visible paint texture and knife marks, sold around $500.

---

## 1. The short answer

Five components, built in strict order, roughly 4,000 lines and eight to ten focused days. Four things only you can provide. When it ships, one collection costs you: one paragraph of intent, one look at the four finished pieces, one click per product to go live. Everything between those three touches runs itself.

The pipeline is currently a manual review tool wearing an autonomous label. The fix is not to bolt automation onto it. It is to insert the three engines that do not exist — upscale, judgement, publication — and to convert your approval from a gate on every item into an exception queue.

Two smaller corrections. Upscaling *any* image produces pixels without detail, so the resolution score alone can be gamed; the detail guard reports measured high-frequency energy against a plain-stretch baseline and never claims "verified" from statistics. And `productSet` with a `customId` identifier makes Shopify writes idempotent at Shopify's end, which removes the duplicate-on-retry problem entirely.

## 3. Your brief, integrated

**The collection.** Four pieces. USA landscapes rendered as the two references do it: layered geometric planes, mirrored reflections, high-key pinks, oranges, teals and magentas, and above all a *physical* surface — impasto, palette-knife ridges, visible canvas weave, transparent glazes over opaque strokes. One centrepiece and three supporting works composed as a single wall. The old library's "Japandi quiet luxury" and "Giclée on Hahnemühle" vocabulary is deleted, not adapted.

**Style lock.** One visual identity chosen once and inherited by all four pieces: a `--sref` code on the Midjourney path, a style-anchor plate on the FLUX path. I will use your references to *write the brief* but will generate our own anchor plate and lock to that, so every piece descends from an image we made rather than from another artist's painting. Style is not copyrightable; provenance still matters for a brand.

**Texture is a scored axis.** The judge's material-realism axis is rewritten to reward what you asked for: visible knife marks, ridge shadows, paint body. A smooth digital gradient scores low on this collection even when it is pretty. The upscaler is set to Topaz's High Fidelity mode rather than its CGI mode, because CGI is tuned for clean renders and would flatten exactly the texture you want kept.

**$500 means a physical print.** A digital file at $500 has no scarcity a customer can feel, and the verified premium band for downloads tops out near $28. But your store already carries a 450-row print ladder at $90–$470 through Gelato, and Gelato is already listed in the pipeline's systems. The coherent product is: the 16.7 MP master feeds a large-format giclée limited edition at $500, mid sizes below it, and a digital tier around $45–$95 for people who frame their own. This is also why print resolution, not download resolution, is the standard the whole pipeline now builds to. **Confirm this reading before I anchor pricing to it.**

**Generation path.** Midjourney has no API. Every prompt is copied by hand and every result URL pasted back, forever. That cannot be made autonomous by any amount of code. FLUX.2 through the key you already hold is end-to-end automatic, accepts up to eight reference images for style conditioning, and renders impasto convincingly when asked. **I recommend FLUX as the production path** and Midjourney only if you decide its look is worth a permanent manual step per image.

## 5. The seams

Independently designed components break where they meet. These are the five contracts, fixed now.

- **Collection → everything.** One record in `data/collections.json` holds the thesis, ratio family, style lock (`sref` or plate hash), version pin and frozen style clauses. Every downstream module reads it; none may override it.
- **Upscale → judge.** The judge receives the master path plus the detail report and refuses to score anything that is not a master. It never sees the 4 MP original.
- **Judge → memory → compiler.** Reason codes are a closed enum. They are written to the creative memory by both the judge *and* the owner's manual rejections, so the loop closes on the right wire either way. The compiler reads the avoid-list at brief time.
- **Judge → pricing.** The composite score enters pricing as a multiplier. Pricing never runs without it on a judged item, and never blocks on it for an unjudged one.
- **Pricing → publisher.** The publisher publishes exactly the price the approval recorded. It does not recompute.

## 6. What you still touch after it ships

Once per collection: write the thesis paragraph; look at the four finished pieces once; on the Midjourney path only, run the plate prompt and paste the code back. Once per product: click "make live". Every ninety days: run the comparables refresh script and read the diff. That is the whole operator workload. The first collection carries more, because the judge runs in shadow mode until it has seen enough of your decisions to be trusted — around thirty items across collections.
